You are working on ThreadsLens, a Chrome (Manifest V3) extension that its owner (Mike) uses on his Mac to study his own Threads feed. Read this whole brief first. If a project folder exists, also read its CLAUDE.md, README.md and test/ before changing anything.

## Purpose
Threads' feed algorithm is tuned for engagement. Mike suspects two things: (1) the feed is shaped or throttled in ways he can't see (for example, in the early morning it leans on older posts from strangers), and (2) many accounts around him are bots, parked or aged "reserve" accounts, or engagement farms (new low-follower accounts, join-month clusters such as Nov 2024 and Nov 2025, accounts that like his posts with no history). ThreadsLens watches the feed as he reads it and shows: a label on every post, a feed-level reading with a 48-hour history, and a private real/fake estimate for each account with the reasons. Everything is local; nothing leaves the browser.

## Ground rules (decided; do not relitigate)
- Passive and human-paced. Read what is on screen as he reads. Never scroll, click or navigate his session for him, except two user-initiated actions in the Post-activity dialog (below). Background profile lookups are OFF by default (the "User glance" toggle) and are slow and jittered.
- Signals, not verdicts, on posts and in the panel. The profile-page card is the one place with an explicit estimate (Likely fake / Likely real / Unclear / Not enough data) with a confidence and reasons in both directions. It is a private estimate: never build shareable lists of named accounts, never state "bot" as fact about a real person in UI copy, never attribute anything to state actors ("coordinated, motivation unclear" at most).
- Age is not trust (accounts get aged and resold). "Real" must be earned by several kinds of evidence. His own rating overrides.
- One user rating per account: `real`, -3..+3. An "interesting" axis was built and removed (he just unfollows); do not re-add it.
- Local only: chrome.storage.local with unlimitedStorage, no network except same-origin profile fetches, no telemetry. Not for the Web Store; at most 2-3 friends via a zip, no support. Desktop Chrome only (the Threads phone app can't be seen by an extension; a Safari/iOS port was scoped and declined).
- Be honest about uncertainty and verify claims before reporting them (two findings had to be retracted; see the lessons).
- The GitHub repo (sniglet/ThreadsLens) is PUBLIC: never commit real third-party Threads handles, post text or exports; work on branches and open pull requests for Mike to merge; ask before force-pushing, deleting or changing visibility; never handle tokens.

## Architecture
- manifest.json: content script (score.js, then content.js) on threads.com and threads.net; permissions storage + unlimitedStorage. Version lives in the manifest (currently 0.5.1).
- score.js: pure functions on window.TLScore (no DOM, no network): scorePost, scoreAccount, judgeAccount, postTelltale, feedState, cohortSpikes, topicShare, cleanBody, cleanBio, isUnavailable, pageContext, isFeedCtx, monthsAgo. All lexicons and thresholds live here.
- content.js: everything with a DOM: extraction (SEL / extract / metric), scan + MutationObserver, IntersectionObserver read detection, storage (store / save / prune), the per-post pill, the re-categorize menu, the overlay panel and 48 h graph, the profile-page card, About-sheet join-date capture, Post-activity dialog capture, slow lookups, exports and import, memory stats.
- test/smoke.js (run `node test/smoke.js`; scoring behaviour) and test/stress/ (a fake virtualized feed plus a WeakRef leak check; README inside).

## What it does
1. Observation. A MutationObserver (500 ms debounce, 1.5 s ceiling) scans `[data-pressable-container="true"]` containers. For each new post it extracts: id and handle (from `/@handle/post/ID` links); body text (the longest span after cleanBody strips Threads chrome such as "14h", "12h12 hours ago", MM/DD/YY, "Replying to ...", "Replied to ... in your thread"; "Post not available" posts are skipped); posted time (`time[datetime]`); like/reply/repost counts (the selectors do not match real Threads yet, so counts are empty); and followed state (a Follow control inside the post means NOT followed; on the home feed no control means followed; elsewhere unknown; re-checked for 5 s after arrival because the badge can render late). pageContext(pathname) tags each post home / thread / profile / activity / search / other. The Activity (notifications) page is skipped entirely, and only `home` posts drive the feed readings. A post counts as "read" after being at least 60% visible for 1.5 s. His own handle is auto-detected (the owner of a Post-activity dialog that shows Insights) and excluded from feed readings.

2. Per-post telltale chip (home feed only, rated as the post arrives; label and reasons stored). One label per post, priority Shaped > Emotional > Throttled > Baseline > Normal, else Neutral (a label needs score >= 0.4):
   - Shaped: wording nearly identical (3-word-shingle Jaccard >= 0.6) to another author's recent post (only later copies are flagged), or the author's account is flagged.
   - Emotional: charged language (>= 2 words from a charged lexicon, or 1 plus >40% caps or >= 2 "!"), heavy arguing underneath (replies/likes > 0.5 at >= 20 likes; needs counts), or non-template engagement bait.
   - Throttled: older than 24 h AND from an account he does not follow (score 0.5 at 24 h rising to 1.0 at 4 days). Old posts from followed accounts are Normal.
   - Baseline: he has seen this exact post in an earlier feed view (more than 10 min earlier), or a non-followed author already had >= 2 of his last 40 arrivals.
   - Normal: from a followed account (fresh scores higher). Neutral: a fresh post from a stranger with nothing unusual.
   The chip is a button: clicking it opens a menu of the six labels (the automatic one marked) plus Undo. His choice wins, shows with a pencil and a white ring, counts in the bars, survives the Follow-badge re-check, is saved as `rec.tell.u`, and is exported as `telltale_user` beside the automatic `telltale`. These corrections are the ground truth for tuning thresholds.
   Bait is a separate golden BAIT chip (pulsing via ONE shared timer). Kinds: comment-farm, opinion hook, giveaway / follow-for-follow, hype / urgency, relatable template ("me when", "POV:"), and a question followed by a pointing emoji.

3. Feed panel (top right; click to expand). Five bars (Normal, Baseline, Throttled, Emotional, Shaped). Each bar is the share of his last 30 read home posts carrying that label divided by a saturation constant (normal 0.5, baseline 0.25, throttled 0.4, emotional 0.2, shaped 0.2), and needs at least 10 reads. Shaped also rises when one topic word dominates the window. The headline names the strongest anomaly bar that is >= 0.4, otherwise Normal, otherwise Mixed. (Baseline reads "Reticulating splines - continue scrolling"; throttled reads "Possibly throttled (low confidence)".) A 48-hour graph in the same colours (15-minute buckets, sampled every 5 minutes). Expanded view: evidence lines (label counts, followed share, distinct authors, median age, top topic word), a memory line, toggles (Rate each post, User glance), buttons (Export CSV, Export DOM sample, Backup JSON, Import backup, Clear data), and the likers tools when a Post-activity dialog is open.

4. Accounts. scoreAccount drives dots and bars: +0.25 watched join cohort (2025-11, 2024-11); +0.15 account <= 3 months old; +0.2 more than 500 threads but under 100 followers; +0.1 under 20 followers; +0.15 auto-style handle (>= 4 trailing digits); +0.05 empty bio (only from a real lookup); +0.2 join-month spike among accounts he sees; +0.15 if >= 40% of >= 3 of their posts he saw are bait. Flag >= 0.5, watch >= 0.25, unknown without data. His rating shifts the score 0.2 per step, forces OK at >= +2 and flag at <= -2.
   judgeAccount (the profile card) is two-sided: weighted evidence for fake and for real, each with a plain reason. Fake if net >= 0.35 (needs >= 2 kinds of evidence); real only if net <= -0.30 AND >= 3 kinds; his +/-2 overrides; confidence low / medium / high by breadth and margin; plus a "to sharpen this" list of missing data. Signals: age (weak evidence for real), followers and threads, bio, handle shape, bait / duplicate / emotional / one-topic share of their posts, likes on his inspected posts with a tiny audience, and activity vs age (an account >= 6 months old with <= 5 threads+replies ever is a strong fake signal, stronger at >= 12 months, and its age credit is dropped; pure lurkers look identical, which the card says).
   Activity counts come from the profile's Threads / Replies / Reposts tabs as he clicks them (tab taken from the URL; a count is "complete" only when the tab says "No ... yet" or a short list stops changing for 4 s). Join date is captured only when he opens "About this profile" (any dialog, or any ancestor of a bare "Joined" label; "Joined October 2026 - 100M+", where the meaning of "100M+" is unknown and stored raw). Followers come from page text or a lookup; bios are cleaned of og:description artifacts.

5. Post-activity dialog (the likes list on his own post). Rows show a handle, a relative time ("35m", assumed to be when they liked), a name and a Follow button. Capture is passive while he scrolls. Two user-initiated buttons: "Scan likers" (scrolls only that dialog at a human pace, about 55-75% of a screen every 1.4-3 s, until the end, 600 accounts or Stop, and waits while the tab is hidden) and "Look up these accounts" (one profile fetch every 20-45 s, at most 90 an hour, 15-minute backoff on 429/5xx). Panel output: per-owner liker stats (flagged / watch / recent / join-spike share, captured vs total likes), like timing (median delay, share in the first hour), late likes from like-count snapshots, top suspects.

6. Lookups ("User glance", off by default). One same-origin profile fetch every 40-110 s while he is actively scrolling, at most 25 an hour, only for authors he read or whose likes list he opened; og:description gives followers, threads and bio.

7. Data and export. Store under chrome.storage.local key "tl": posts{id}, accounts{handle}, reads[], likers{owner/post}, history[], settings. Bounded: 4,000 posts, 6,000 accounts (rated, joined, tab-counted and liker accounts are kept), 80 liker sets, 500 reads, 48 h of history. Saving is debounced with a 20 s ceiling and flushed on pagehide. Exports: posts CSV (id, handle, kind, text, posted, likes, replies, reposts, followed, read, tags, seen, ctx, telltale, telltale_why, telltale_user); accounts CSV (handle, joined, followers, threads, bio, level, score, verdict, confidence, your_rating, reasons, threads_seen, replies_seen, reposts_seen); likers CSV; a structure-only DOM sample (no post text or usernames) for fixing selectors; and a full backup JSON with a merge import (existing values win, the backup fills gaps).

## Engineering rules learned the hard way
- Memory and performance: never hold page elements in long-lived structures (WeakSet only; unobserve and clear timers when nodes are removed); no per-element animations (an infinite Element.animate() kept every removed post alive); cache scorePost per post; cap every collection; never sort the whole store per call. Prove leaks with test/stress (WeakRef + memory pressure, with the old behaviour as a control); performance.memory heap numbers cannot show a leak.
- Do not call save() on every tick of a loop (it starves the debounce).
- Verify before reporting. "Date-only posts" like 09/18/26 were Threads' timestamp on older posts, not content; most "emotional" hits were caps/exclamation enthusiasm; the first feed graph was an artifact of followed-state and profile-visit bugs.
- Edits once vanished from content.js: after editing, grep for the feature's identifiers, run `node --check content.js score.js` and `node test/smoke.js`.
- Test DOM code with static fixtures (directory names like @handle/post/ID so location.pathname matches) in the Claude browser pane: stub window.chrome, then eval score.js and content.js in the page. The pane is hidden (override document.visibilityState, use an instant scrollBy, poll instead of waiting for scroll events, mind the 45 s tool timeout). Real Threads markup has never been seen by these tests.
- After every change tell Mike to reload the extension at chrome://extensions AND refresh the Threads tab.
- Mike is a hardware / iOS developer: give direct answers, honest uncertainty, and the next step, not a survey of options.

## Calibration facts (about 2,100 real posts over 3 days; do not re-derive)
On the home-like feed: Neutral 46%, Normal 45%, Throttled 6% (12% in the morning vs 4% the rest of the day, almost all from non-followed accounts), Emotional 1%, Baseline and Shaped about 0%. Median post age is 9.6 h in the morning (5-9am) vs 2.2 h otherwise; the effect showed on 2 of 3 mornings, so it is suggestive only. Looked-up accounts are mostly established (median 722 followers; 5% under 20 followers). His ratings are 59 positive (mostly famous accounts) vs 8 negative: too few and too biased to fit a model.

## Known gaps and unverified assumptions
